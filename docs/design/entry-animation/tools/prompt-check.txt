BEFORE YOU FINISH, CHECK EVERY ONE OF THESE; each one has gone wrong before, and an image that fails any of them is rejected. 1. THE SWORD IS ONE RIGID STRAIGHT BAR: the pommel, the hilt, the guard, the blade and the tip all lie on ONE single straight line. A ruler laid along the blade runs exactly down the middle of the hilt to the pommel cap. There is NO angle and NO kink at the guard or at the hands, and the hilt on the two sides of a fist lies on one line, never offset. With one hand on the sword that hand is next to the guard, never at the pommel end. This holds when the sword is raised or swung overhead too: never tilt the hilt one way while the blade hangs straight down another way. 2. A SHEATHED SWORD IS ALSO ONE STRAIGHT BAR: the hilt, the guard and the scabbard lie on one straight line, and where his body hides part of it, the two visible parts line up exactly, as if the bar passed straight behind him. The same goes for the EMPTY scabbard: its open collar and its tail lie on one straight line. 3. The blade and the scabbard have no curve at all. 4. The hilt is just long enough for two fists side by side, the same in every image, and the blade reaches from the top of his head to his hips, the same in every image; only foreshortening may shorten them. The hilt is the same length whether one hand or two hands hold it: it never grows to make room for the second hand. Never shrink the blade or stretch it to fit the image: if it does not fit, make the figure smaller or move it. 5. Follow each arm from its shoulder to its hand: the sword hand is the RIGHT hand, on the RIGHT arm. The hand next to the guard is ALWAYS the RIGHT hand: facing the viewer it belongs to the arm on the viewer's LEFT; facing right it belongs to the arm nearest to the viewer; facing left it belongs to the arm whose shoulder is on the viewer's left. 6. Two-handed grip: the RIGHT hand is next to the guard and the LEFT hand is at the pommel end, the two fists side by side, touching but not overlapping, with only the pommel cap showing beyond the left fist; and neither goes past the guard. Of two hands on one hilt only ONE shows its back with the glove star; the other shows its fingers. THE STAR RULE, facing right: the hand that shows the glove star is the near RIGHT hand and it TOUCHES THE GUARD, on the hilt side of it. On a drawn sword the other hand is at the pommel cap. On a sheathed sword the other hand is on the scabbard, on the other side of the guard. Never put the star hand at the pommel end or on the scabbard. 7. Facing right he is a pure side view with his chest NOT toward the viewer, so his navel cannot be seen, and his arms reach forward side by side, never crossed. Only facing left or facing the viewer may the chest be seen. 8. While the sword is sheathed the round guard is BETWEEN his two hands: the RIGHT hand is on the wrapped hilt on the pommel side of the guard, and the hilt is never left empty. The hand on the scabbard is always the LEFT hand, in front of his belly, at the scabbard mouth, touching the guard with its thumb; never a dark blob, never behind his back. It must read as a LEFT hand: facing right we see its palm side, with four bare fingertips curling up from under the scabbard, the thumb along the top, and no glove star; facing left we see the back of the hand with its star. 9. There are exactly two hands in the image: count them, and give every finger a hand and every hand an arm; there is no third set of fingers on the hilt. With one hand on the sword the hilt does not droop away from the blade: the pommel stays on the line of the blade. Every open hand has a thumb and four fingers. A hand holding nothing is closed into a firm fist. 10. While sheathing, the bare blade goes straight into the open scabbard mouth: the blade and the scabbard are one straight line, and the rest of the blade is inside. The scabbard has its parts in the right order: the red cord is only at the mouth end, next to the guard or the open collar; the far end is the tail, with the silver end cap and no red cord. Exactly one sword and one scabbard. Exactly ONE solid blade: ghost edges in a streak are thin pale lines, never a second solid dark blade. The hilt from the guard to the pommel is one continuous wrap, with no silver ring in the middle. Once the sword is drawn the scabbard is empty: it ends in a plain open silver collar, with no hilt and no guard on it. 11. If this image has a motion streak at all, it is one clean sweep that ENDS EXACTLY AT THE BLADE TIP: it never continues past the tip and never hooks back around. It is long, flat and fast: a shallow arc or a nearly straight line, never a round crescent, unless the move is a full spin.
