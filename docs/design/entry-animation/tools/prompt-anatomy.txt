HUMAN ANATOMY, correct like a real person drawn from life: one head, one torso, two arms, two legs. Each arm has one shoulder, one elbow, one wrist and one hand; the upper arm and the forearm are the same length and do not stretch. Elbows and knees bend only in their natural direction, knees point the same way as the toes, and each ankle is straight in line with its shin. Hips, ribcage and shoulders turn together believably, and each leg ends in one foot on the correct side, the toes pointing away from the heel. BOTH ARMS ARE VISIBLE: every hand in the image is attached to a forearm we can see from elbow to wrist; no fingers appear on any object without their whole hand and forearm. HANDS: one RIGHT hand and one LEFT hand, a mirrored pair, never two copies of the same hand. A gripping hand has four fingers wrapped around the object from one side and the thumb closing over them from the other side, five digits in all, the fingers evenly lined up. SWORD: the blade and the scabbard are PERFECTLY STRAIGHT, ruler straight, exactly as on the sword sheet; there is exactly one sword and exactly one scabbard. The hilt passes through the closed fist across the palm: the blade comes out of the thumb side of the fist and the pommel comes out of the little-finger side. The hilt never touches or passes through the wrist or the forearm: the forearm and the hilt form a clear angle of about 120 degrees. HE IS RIGHT-HANDED IN EVERY IMAGE: the sword is drawn and held by the RIGHT hand; the scabbard is worn on the LEFT hip, in FRONT of nothing else and never behind his back; the left hand never reaches behind his back. NEVER A MIRROR IMAGE: when he faces the RIGHT edge of the image, the hip nearest to us is his RIGHT hip and nothing hangs on it, and the scabbard is on the far side of his body; when he faces the LEFT edge, the hip nearest to us is his LEFT hip and the scabbard hangs there in plain view. CONSISTENCY: the character, clothes, boots, sword and scabbard are copied exactly from the model sheet and the sword sheet in the reference images. The blade from the guard to the tip is exactly as long as the distance from the top of his head to his hips, and the hilt is about one third of that, just long enough for two fists side by side, in every image; only foreshortening may change how long it looks. Both hands fit on the hilt between the guard and the pommel; no hand goes past the guard toward the blade. BLADE EDGE: the sword has ONE cutting edge, exactly as on the sword sheet: a bright silver edge along one side and a plain dark back along the other side; the point of the tip lies on the back side and the edge rises in a short slant to meet it. The cutting edge always faces the direction the blade is moving and cutting; the back of the blade trails behind it, on the thumb side of the grip. While the sword is sheathed its edge faces up. DRAWN SWORD: once the sword is drawn the scabbard is empty: only the open scabbard mouth shows, with no hilt, no guard and no second blade on it. The scabbard stays attached at the hip through the sash, and never passes through the body.
